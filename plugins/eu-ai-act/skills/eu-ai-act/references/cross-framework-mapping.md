# Cross-framework mapping — EU AI Act to other frameworks

There is **no ISO 42001 control A.5.8**. Human oversight maps to **A.9.2**. **A.5** is impact assessment (FRIA analogue). **A.5.5** is **societal impacts**, not documentation. Documentation maps to **A.6.2.7** and **A.8**. **A.6** is the AI system life cycle. **A.10** is suppliers and customers.

## Requirement-level mapping

| EU AI Act requirement | ISO 42001 control | NIST AI RMF subcategory | South Korea AI Act | Brazil AI Act | NYC LL144 |
|-----------------------|-------------------|------------------------|-------------------|---------------|-----------|
| Risk classification (Art. 6, Annex III) | **A.5** impact assessment informs scope; **A.6** life cycle once in scope | MAP-1 (context established), MAP-2 (AI risks and benefits mapped) | High-impact AI classification (not EU-style high-risk) | Risk-based tiered classification (pending bill — not enacted) | AEDT determination — whether the tool is an automated employment decision tool |
| Fundamental rights impact assessment (Art. 27) | **A.5** (A.5.2–A.5.5) AISIA analogue. **A.5.5 = societal impacts**, not documentation | MAP 5 impacts | Art. 35 endeavor + public-procurement preference (not a mandatory FRIA) | Algorithmic impact assessment for high-risk AI (Senate substitute; not law) | Employment disparate-impact tables only |
| Risk management system (Art. 9) | Clause 6.1.2 / 8.2 AI risk assessment; treatment 6.1.3; life-cycle operation **A.6** | GOVERN (governance structure), MAP (risk identification), MANAGE (risk treatment) | Operator risk-management measures | Algorithmic impact assessment for high-risk AI | No direct equivalent — limited to bias-audit scope |
| Data and data governance (Art. 10) | **A.7** Data for AI systems (quality, provenance, preparation; A.7.5 provenance) | MEASURE ME-1 (appropriate methods and metrics) | Data quality and management requirements for AI training | Data governance provisions including quality, accuracy, and relevance | No direct equivalent — data requirements implicit in bias-audit methodology |
| Technical documentation (Art. 11, Annex IV) | **A.6.2.7** technical documentation; **A.8** information for interested parties (A.8.2 user information) | MAP MAP-5 (documentation of system characteristics) | Documentation requirements for high-impact AI systems | Documentation obligations for AI system providers | No direct equivalent — summary results published but no technical-documentation requirement |
| Record-keeping and logging (Art. 12) | **A.6.2.8** recording of event logs | MEASURE ME-2 (monitoring and tracking) | Logging and audit-trail requirements | Record-keeping provisions for AI system decisions | Audit records must be retained but no real-time logging requirement |
| Transparency and information to deployers (Art. 13) | **A.8** information for interested parties (A.8.2 system documentation and information for users) | GOVERN GV-1.2 (documentation and communication) | Transparency obligations including right to explanation | Right to information about AI system use; right to explanation of decisions | Notice requirement — employers must notify candidates/employees 10 business days before use |
| Human oversight (Art. 14) | **A.9.2** processes for responsible use (human oversight). **Not A.5.8** (that ID does not exist). Life-cycle gates also in A.6.1.3 / A.6.2.5–A.6.2.6 | MANAGE MG-2 (human decision-making retained) | Human-oversight provisions; right to request human review | Right to human review of AI-assisted decisions | Alternative selection procedure — candidates may request an alternative process |
| Accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity (Art. 15) | **A.6.2.4** verification and validation; operation **A.6.2.6** | MEASURE ME-1 (performance metrics), MANAGE MG-3 (risk responses) | Performance and reliability standards for AI systems | Accuracy and reliability requirements | No direct equivalent — accuracy implicitly addressed through bias-audit statistical analysis |
| Bias and fairness (Art. 10(2)(f)) | **A.5.4** impact on individuals or groups; **A.5.5** societal impacts (not a documentation control) | MEASURE ME-3 (fairness and bias evaluation) | Non-discrimination obligations; fairness requirements | Non-discrimination provisions; prohibition of discriminatory outcomes | Bias audit requirement — annual independent audit for disparate impact by race/ethnicity and sex |
| Incident reporting (Art. 73) | **A.8.4** communication of incidents; **A.8.3** external reporting | MANAGE MG-4 (incident response) | Incident reporting to competent authorities | Incident reporting obligations for serious harm | No incident reporting requirement under LL144 |
| Post-market monitoring (Art. 72) | **A.6.2.6** operation and monitoring | MEASURE (ongoing measurement and monitoring) | Ongoing monitoring requirements for high-impact AI | Continuous monitoring provisions | Annual bias audit constitutes periodic monitoring (limited scope) |
| Conformity assessment (Art. 43, Annexes VI–VII) | Clause 9 performance evaluation; Clause 10 improvement — **not** a CE-marking analogue | No direct equivalent — NIST is a voluntary framework | Certification and conformity requirements under development | Conformity assessment provisions for high-risk AI | No conformity assessment — compliance demonstrated through published bias audit |
| Value chain / suppliers and customers (Arts. 25, 28) | **A.10** third-party and customer relationships (A.10.2 allocation of responsibilities, A.10.3 suppliers, A.10.4 customers) | GOVERN 6, MANAGE 3 | Domestic representative for foreign operators | Distributor and value-chain roles | Vendor may commission the audit; **employer remains liable** |

## Compliance gap analysis across frameworks

| Gap area | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| Conformity assessment and CE marking | Unique to the EU AI Act — no equivalent in NIST AI RMF, ISO 42001 (certifiable but different), or NYC LL144. Art. 43 point-1 path is a **choice** of Annex VI or VII when Art. 40/41 instruments are fully applied |
| GPAI-specific obligations | Only the EU AI Act has dedicated GPAI/foundation-model rules (Chapter V), including Art. 55(1)(b) Union-level systemic-risk assessment as a distinct duty from red-teaming |
| Extraterritorial enforcement with penalties | The EU AI Act combines extraterritorial scope with binding penalties. NIST AI RMF is voluntary; ISO 42001 is certifiable but not legally binding; NYC LL144 is jurisdiction-limited |
| Fundamental rights impact assessment | Article 27 FRIA is a deployer duty with a gated addressee. ISO 42001 **A.5** AISIA is the closest analogue but is organisation-wide and not limited to public bodies / 5(b)–(c) |
| Prohibited practices | Only the EU AI Act defines outright bans (Art. 5, including purposefully manipulative or deceptive techniques in Art. 5(1)(a)). Other frameworks use risk-proportionate approaches without absolute prohibitions |
| Supply-chain obligations | The EU AI Act creates cascading obligations from GPAI providers to AI system providers to deployers (Arts. 25, 28, 53). ISO 42001 **A.10** covers suppliers and customers |
